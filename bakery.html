Our Craft

Everything folded by hand, nothing hurried.

Maison Auguste began as a single proofing cabinet in the back of a friend's café. Today it's still four bakers, one dough table, and a rule we've never broken: butter is folded in by hand, in full sheets, three times over — never machine-laminated, never frozen.

We mill a portion of our own flour, keep a hundred-year-old levain alive around the clock, and bake in small batches through the night so the first croissant out of the oven is never more than a few hours old when it reaches you.

The Process

Four stages.
No shortcuts.

Every laminated pastry we sell passes through the same four stages, in the same order, every single time.

01

Mix & Ferment

Flour, water, and levain are mixed at dusk and left to ferment slowly overnight at a cool room temperature.

02

Laminate

A block of Isigny butter is folded into the dough in three single turns, by hand, with a full rest between each.

03

Shape & Proof

Cut and rolled just before sunrise, then proofed a final time until the layers turn light and visibly separated.

04

Bake to Order

Small trays go into the deck oven through the morning, so nothing sits under the lamp for more than an hour.
